The hill test

Nm ÷ tonne

Engine size doesn't predict hill behaviour — torque against weight does. Both numbers are in every listing's spec tab.

127 Nm / tonne Fine
Under 115 — struggles, aircon on and loaded 115–125 — marginal, you'll notice it 125–135 — fine in normal driving 135+ — pulls up hills without downshifting
Hard no

AMT gearboxes. Badged AGS (Suzuki), Easy-R (Renault / Datsun), or just AMT. An automated manual hesitates and lurches going uphill — the worst possible transmission for your one stated problem. Manual is best; a proper auto or CVT is fine.

Shortcut: any 1.0 turbo beats a bigger naturally-aspirated engine on hills. Look for TSI, TCe, T-GDI, or "turbo" in the title.

The mileage test

km ÷ year

Raw odometer numbers mean nothing without the age. 90 000 km on a 2018 is unremarkable; on a 2024 it's a rental fleet car.

— km / year —
Under 8 000 — suspiciously low, see below 8 000–15 000 — low and healthy 15 000–25 000 — normal SA average 25 000–35 000 — high, expect more wear 35 000+ — almost certainly ex-rental or a rep car

Age is estimated from the model year, so treat it as approximate — check the registration date on the papers.

The counter-intuitive one

Very low mileage isn't automatically good. A car that stood for long stretches, or only ever did short cold trips, can be in worse shape than a high-mileage highway car — perished seals, tired battery, oil never reaching temperature, brake discs pitted with rust. If it's under 8 000 km/year, ask why, and check the service history was still done on time intervals rather than km.

On the test drive

In person
The one that matters most: find a real hill, put the aircon on full, and take a passenger. That's the exact condition an underpowered car hides from the spec sheet. If it's fine like that, it's fine.

About the money

The one that decides it

"Can I take it to my own mechanic for a pre-purchase inspection?"

The answer matters more than the report. A confident dealer says yes without blinking. Hesitation, excuses, or "our technicians have already checked it" is a walk-away — not because the car is definitely bad, but because you'll never know. An independent DEKRA or AA inspection runs about R1 000–R1 500 and is the best money in this entire process. It's the first line in your drive-away table — and budget to pay it twice if the first car you inspect fails.

At the desk

Negotiating
Never do this

Don't tell them your monthly budget. Say "R3 000 a month" and they'll hit it — by stretching the term and quietly leaving the price where it is. Negotiate the price of the car, always. The instalment is an output, not the thing you're buying.

Before you sign

Contract
Makes or breaks the plan

Get it in writing that extra payments reduce capital. Many banks default to treating overpayment as paying instalments in advance — your account shows "paid ahead", you feel great, and interest keeps accruing on the full balance. Then check your statement after month two to confirm the capital actually dropped by more than scheduled.

Your rate, realistically

Thin file

A middling score with no previous loans isn't a bad score — it's a thin file. The number reflects an absence of evidence, not bad behaviour. Credit scores reward demonstrated repayment history, and paying cash for a previous car means none was ever generated.

Practically, expect roughly prime + 2% to prime + 3.5% rather than the best-tier rate. Confirm the current prime rate when you apply — it moves.

Your 36% deposit is the thing that offsets it. Loan-to-value is a major input, and no balloon plus a big deposit makes you a low-risk application regardless of file thickness.

What actually helps, in order

  • Pull your free credit reports first. One free report a year from each bureau — TransUnion, Experian, Compuscan, XDS. Errors are common and a dispute takes a week or two. Do this before any application.
  • Apply to your own bank first. They can see years of your salary and spending, which partly substitutes for the missing loan history. A stranger bank can't.
  • Keep applications inside one fortnight. Spread over months they read as distress; clustered they read as normal rate-shopping. With a thin file, don't apply to six places.
  • Don't let the approval amount move your target. They'll likely approve more than R129 000. That's what they'll lend, not what you should spend.
